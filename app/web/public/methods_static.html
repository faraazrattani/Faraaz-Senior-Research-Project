Research Methodology

A comprehensive overview of our machine learning approach to earnings week stock prediction

1. Research Objective

The primary goal of this research is to predict stock price movements during earnings announcement weeks using machine learning techniques. We focus specifically on the direction of price movement (up or down) rather than magnitude, as this is often more practical for trading strategies.

Research Question

Can machine learning models effectively predict the direction of stock price movements during earnings weeks using historical market data and technical indicators?

2. Data Sources & Collection

2.1 Stock Price Data

We collect daily Open, High, Low, Close, and Volume (OHLCV) data using the yfinance Python library. This provides access to Yahoo Finance's historical data going back several decades for most major stocks.

Data Specifications:

  • Frequency: Daily bars
  • History: Minimum 5 years, up to 10 years when available
  • Universe: S&P 500 constituents and major tech stocks
  • Adjustments: Split and dividend adjusted prices

2.2 Earnings Data

Earnings announcement dates and surprise metrics are collected from multiple sources with fallback mechanisms:

# Primary: yfinance earnings calendar # Fallback: Alpha Vantage API (requires key) # Fallback: Finnhub API (requires key)

2.3 Data Quality & Preprocessing

All data undergoes validation and cleaning procedures to ensure quality:

Missing Data

Forward-fill missing values, drop stocks with >5% missing data in analysis window

Outlier Detection

Remove price movements >50% in single day (likely data errors or corporate actions)

Corporate Actions

Use split-adjusted prices; exclude earnings windows affected by major M&A or spinoffs

Survivorship Bias

Include delisted stocks where data is available to avoid survivorship bias

3. Feature Engineering

Our feature set combines technical indicators, fundamental metrics, and market context variables. All features are calculated using only information available at the time of prediction to avoid look-ahead bias.

3.1 Technical Indicators

Price Returns

1-day, 5-day, 10-day, and 20-day returns relative to earnings announcement

Moving Averages

SMA(20), SMA(50), SMA(200) and current price gaps relative to these levels

Momentum Indicators

RSI(14), MACD(12,26,9), and momentum oscillator values

Volatility

20-day rolling volatility, volatility rank, and average true range

3.2 Fundamental Features

Earnings History

Historical earnings surprise %, beat/miss ratio, surprise trend

Volume Patterns

Volume ratios, average volume, volume trend leading up to earnings

Market Context

Sector performance, market correlation, relative strength vs SPY

Seasonality

Month of year, quarter, days since last earnings announcement

3.3 Feature Engineering Pipeline

Feature_t = f(Price_{t-n:t}, Volume_{t-n:t}, Earnings_history, Market_context_t)

Where t represents the day before earnings announcement (T-1) and n varies by feature type (typically 1-252 days).

4. Label Definition & Target Variable

The target variable represents the direction of stock price movement during the "earnings window" - a period surrounding the earnings announcement.

Label Calculation

y = sign(Close_{T+2} - Close_{T-1})

Where T is the earnings announcement date. We use T-1 to T+2 to capture:

  • Pre-announcement positioning effects
  • Initial market reaction
  • Post-announcement adjustment period

4.1 Alternative Label Definitions Considered

Label Type Definition Pros Cons
Binary Direction sign(T+2 - T-1) Simple, tradeable Ignores magnitude
Magnitude Bins |T+2 - T-1| buckets Captures size of moves Unbalanced classes
Excess Return (T+2 - T-1) - Market Market-neutral Complex interpretation

5. Machine Learning Models

5.1 Model Selection

We implement multiple model architectures to compare performance and robustness:

Random Forest

Baseline ensemble method with built-in feature importance and good interpretability

XGBoost

Gradient boosting for enhanced performance with careful hyperparameter tuning

LSTM (Optional)

Recurrent neural network for capturing temporal dependencies in price sequences

Logistic Regression

Linear baseline for comparison and coefficient interpretation

5.2 Model Training & Validation

We use time-series cross-validation to respect the temporal nature of financial data:

Cross-Validation Strategy

  • Training Window: 2 years of historical data
  • Validation Window: 6 months forward testing
  • Step Size: 3 months (rolling window)
  • Gap: 1 week between train and validation to prevent leakage

6. Evaluation Metrics

Given the financial application, we focus on metrics relevant to trading performance rather than just statistical accuracy.

6.1 Classification Metrics

Metric Formula Interpretation
Accuracy (TP + TN) / (TP + TN + FP + FN) Overall correctness, baseline ~50%
Precision TP / (TP + FP) When we predict "up", how often right?
Recall TP / (TP + FN) Of actual "up" moves, how many caught?
F1-Score 2 * (Precision * Recall) / (Precision + Recall) Harmonic mean of precision and recall

6.2 Financial Metrics

Directional Accuracy

Percentage of correct directional predictions (most important for trading)

Profit Factor

Ratio of profits from correct predictions to losses from incorrect ones

Sharpe Ratio

Risk-adjusted returns of a strategy based on model predictions

Maximum Drawdown

Largest peak-to-trough decline in strategy equity curve

7. Backtesting Framework

We implement a comprehensive backtesting system to evaluate both our ML models and traditional strategies on historical data.

7.1 Strategy Implementation

Buy & Hold

Baseline strategy: buy and hold throughout the entire period

SMA Crossover

Long when SMA(50) > SMA(200), short otherwise

Momentum 12-1

Monthly rebalancing based on 12-month minus 1-month returns

Earnings Week ML

Trade around earnings based on ML model predictions

7.2 Transaction Cost Modeling

Cost Structure

  • Commission: $0.005 per share (typical retail)
  • Slippage: 0.05% of trade value (market impact)
  • Spread: 0.02% for liquid stocks (bid-ask spread)
  • Borrowing Cost: 2% annually for short positions

8. Limitations & Assumptions

⚠️ Important Limitations

This research has several important limitations that users must understand:

8.1 Data Limitations

Survivorship Bias

Focus on large-cap stocks may overestimate performance

Look-Ahead Bias

Despite precautions, some features may leak future information

Data Quality

Free data sources may have errors or delays compared to professional feeds

Sample Size

Limited earnings events per stock reduces statistical power

8.2 Model Limitations

Overfitting Risk

Complex models may not generalize to new market conditions

Regime Changes

Models trained on historical data may fail during market regime shifts

Feature Stability

Predictive features may become less relevant over time

Non-Stationarity

Financial markets evolve, making historical patterns less reliable

8.3 Implementation Limitations

  • Backtesting assumes perfect execution at closing prices
  • No account for market impact of large orders
  • Simplified transaction cost model
  • No consideration of funding costs or margin requirements
  • Assumes continuous market liquidity
